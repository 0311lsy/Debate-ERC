Dear Editors of Expert Systems with Applications,

We submit our manuscript "Selective Cross-Model Verification for Emotion
Recognition in Conversations: When and Why Heterogeneous Review Helps" for
consideration as a research article.

Why ESWA. The paper is an applied intelligent-systems study rather than a
pure machine-learning method paper: it answers a deployment question—given an
already-deployed fine-tuned LLM classifier, when should a second model be
consulted, at what cost, and under what structural conditions does the
consultation help? The deliverable is a cost-aware inference policy with
measured operating points (1.27x expected compute, Pareto frontier), hardware
footprint (two 7B models on one 48GB GPU), and a quantified error-reduction
projection for high-volume ERC pipelines. This emphasis on expert-system
deployment, cost-quality trade-offs, and reproducible operating conditions
matches ESWA's scope; the mechanism-only narrative would instead target KBS or
Pattern Recognition.

What is new and verified.
1. A selective cross-model verification framework whose gain is established
   over eight paired seeds with paired t-test, Wilcoxon, and bootstrap
   confidence intervals (p=0.019), and which transfers zero-shot (frozen
   hyperparameters) to IEMOCAP over three seeds (p=0.037).
2. A control ladder isolating the necessary conditions: homogeneous
   self-debate, self-consistency at two temperatures, and a capability-mismatched
   1.5B reviewer all fail, while a 3x3 factorial cross shows the gain is
   multiplicative in reviewer independence and capability parity.
3. A cost-accuracy Pareto characterization and an explicit, honestly reported
   boundary: the rule gate nearly exhausts its available signal, abstention is
   needed for rare classes, and two negative seeds plus a mixed hint ablation
   are reported rather than suppressed.
